Bias Lens

Which words in this article would readers call biased, and how many would agree? Analysed on this board: your text does not leave your home network.

Pair this browser with the board

Everything sent to the board is encrypted with a key only this browser and the board know. To set it up, press Show code, then type the six digits that scroll across the board's LED matrix.

    most readers would flag it readers split Hover over or tap a word to see how many readers would flag it.
    How to read this

    Each highlighted word shows the model's estimate of how many readers in ten would call it biased.

    The estimate comes from MBIC, a dataset in which about ten crowd workers in the US read each of 1,700 news sentences from 2017 to 2020 and marked the words they found biased. The model learned how often those readers agreed, not a single verdict.

    • Readers split marks words that some readers flag and others do not. These words are contested: reasonable readers disagree about them.
    • Most readers would flag it marks words that 6 or more readers in 10 would flag.

    A highlight is a prompt to look again, not a ruling. The model reflects those annotators' judgements of US political news in English. It can miss words that are new or rare, and it can be wrong.

    Privacy: the text is analysed on this board, kept only while it is being analysed, and not sent anywhere.

    Use Bias Lens while you read

    On a laptop: the Chrome or Edge extension

    The Bias Lens extension marks articles on news sites automatically, right on the page. Load it from the project's clients/extension folder (see docs/BIAS_LENS_CLIENTS.md), then connect it to this board's address, , and pair it with a code from the LED matrix.

    On a phone: Chrome

    1. Pair this phone's browser with the board on this page (only once).
    2. Then bookmark any page, edit that bookmark, name it Bias Lens, and paste into its address (URL).
    3. On a news article: tap the address bar, type Bias Lens and tap the bookmark. A board tab opens, reads the article, closes, and the highlights appear on the article. Tap a marked word to see its share.

    On a computer, you can also drag this link to the bookmarks bar: Bias Lens.
